## Robustness assessment of functional enrichment analysis

### 1. Objective
This report evaluates the sensitivity of functional enrichment results to the Ensembl-to-Entrez gene mapping strategy.
The original mapping strategy was compared with a strict one-to-one mapping approach.

### 2. Mapping methodology
The strict mapping retains only Ensembl-Entrez pairs for which both identifiers have exactly one association.
Ambiguous associations were excluded rather than resolved through arbitrary selection.

### 7. Interpretation
The comparison indicates high concordance between the original and strict mapping approaches.
In the siNRP1_3 DOWN Reactome analysis, all four shared pathways retained identical gene membership.
In the siNRP1_5 UP Reactome analysis, gene membership was identical in 55 of 60 shared pathways, with very high overall Jaccard similarity.
The observed differences were associated with the exclusion of ambiguous gene mappings, including VAMP7 and BUB1B-PAK6 in the previous readable gene lists.

### 8. Methodological limitations
- Mapping robustness does not represent independent biological validation.
- The strict mapping reduces gene coverage and may exclude biologically relevant genes.
- Gene membership comparison uses readable gene symbols rather than raw Entrez identifiers.
- Jaccard similarity measures set overlap, not enrichment significance or effect size.
- GO and Reactome terms can overlap biologically and share genes.
- Enrichment does not demonstrate pathway activation, inhibition, or causality.
- The analysis is based on one RNA-seq dataset and its associated experimental design.

### 9. Conclusion
The functional enrichment findings show high methodological robustness to the gene mapping strategy, particularly for the Reactome signatures examined at gene-membership level.
The strict one-to-one mapping provides a more conservative and transparent approach by excluding ambiguous identifier relationships.
